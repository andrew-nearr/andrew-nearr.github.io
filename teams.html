People who shaped my journey

Teams

These are the leaders I once followed and worked alongside. Each achieved remarkable things in his own field. They helped me grow with extraordinary patience and stood beside me at pivotal moments in my life. I remain deeply grateful to them.

New York Real Estate Development

Ben Zhang · 张航东

Ben is my model for how to work. He brought me into New York real estate development and showed me what diligence truly looks like.

Through watching him work, diligence stopped being an abstract virtue and became something tangible. He was instrumental in helping me find my footing and build a life in New York.

About Ben Zhang. Ben Zhang, also known as Hang Dong Zhang, currently focuses primarily on real estate development in New York. His work is grounded in the practical demands of moving development projects forward and turning plans into completed buildings.

A New Chapter in America

Peter Wang · 王祖光

Peter led me across the ocean and into a new chapter in the United States.

He supported me countless times—in daily life, in work, and in financing. His help gave me the confidence and practical foundation to begin again in a new country.

About Peter Wang. Peter Zuguang Wang founded the company that became Cenntro and has served as Cenntro’s Managing Director, Chairman and Chief Executive Officer since its Nasdaq listing in December 2021. Earlier in his career, he co-founded UTStarcom. His work has spanned telecommunications, entrepreneurship and the development of electric commercial vehicles. Cenntro · SEC

Automotive & Industrial Design

Chao Wang · 王超

Chao designed an electric logistics vehicle specifically for the local delivery business I led.

An expert in automotive and industrial design, he was also behind the design of the celebrated Mobike bicycle. Working with him showed me how thoughtful design can turn an operational need into a real product.

About Chao Wang. Chao is the founder and chairman of electric-vehicle maker Kaiyun Motors and was an important designer of the original Mobike. His Mobike work applied ideas from automotive engineering—including airless tires and a single-sided wheel mounting system designed for rapid replacement—to the practical challenge of operating a vast shared-bicycle fleet. The result was a bicycle made to be visually distinctive, durable and easier to maintain at scale. China Daily · China News Service

Mobility & Crisis Leadership

Herman Zhou · 周航

Herman was the leader who brought me into the mobility industry. He founded Yidao Yongche—China’s pioneering ride-hailing company.

While we worked together, his support carried me through many operational crises. Those experiences taught me to become calm inside a crisis, hold the line, and refuse to let the organization collapse.

About Herman Zhou. Herman Hang Zhou founded Yidao Yongche in 2010, making it China’s first ride-hailing company and an early pioneer of app-based mobility before the country’s later ride-hailing boom. The Financial Times, reporting on Uber’s battle for China, featured his perspective on how private-car ride-sharing would evolve under new regulation. His early work helped establish mobility on demand as a real industry in China. Financial Times · Sixth Tone

Writing, Practical Action & Collaboration

Yuanjie Zheng · 郑渊洁

I had the privilege of learning from Yuanjie Zheng face to face. He helped me leave behind the habit of talking in abstractions without grounding ideas in action, and taught me how to work with others to get things done.

One lesson has stayed with me ever since: focus your attention on doing the work well, not on being liked. He told me, “I asked you to work with me because I want us to make something happen—not because I want you to please me. If you do not make me happy in the moment, but you get the work done, I will be happy in the end.”

About Yuanjie Zheng. Known in China as the “King of Fairy Tales,” he created enduring characters including Pipilu, Luxixi, Shuke and Beita. English-language coverage has described his body of work as China’s modern “Grimm’s Fairy Tales.” His magazine King of Fairy Tales—for which he was the sole writer—reached a peak monthly circulation of more than one million copies, and his characters have accompanied generations of Chinese readers. Global Times · Beijing Review
