Reading 01

Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus

逻辑哲学论

This is my favorite book of philosophy. It is difficult, but it has changed how I think about facts, language, and the boundaries of what can honestly be said.

When I first opened the book, I encountered the sentence: “The world is all that is the case.” Soon after comes the distinction that stayed with me: “The world is the totality of facts, not of things.” I suspect that the importance I place on paperwork in both work and life has much to do with this idea. Paperwork turns events, agreements, responsibilities, and ownership into facts that can be identified and examined.

Later, when I learned that my younger daughter would study linguistics at Cambridge, I immediately wanted to recommend this book to her. Few books make the relationship among language, thought, and the world feel so consequential.

The book is not easy to read. Fortunately, we now have AI assistants. They can help us first see the structure—the route through the propositions—before we return to the text and work through it one step at a time. AI cannot do the reading for us, but it can help us find the entrance.

Step by step, I reached the propositions that say: “The limits of my language mean the limits of my world”; “The sense of the world must lie outside the world”; “Not how the world is, is the mystical, but that it is”; “Death is not an event in life: we do not live to experience death”; and finally, “Whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent.”

What I remember most deeply is a principle I draw from the book: What can be stated clearly should be stated clearly. What cannot be stated clearly should not be dressed in language that creates the illusion that it has already been understood.

I also remember Wittgenstein’s ladder: after using the propositions to climb beyond them, the reader must throw the ladder away. The book does not hand us a final collection of doctrines. It teaches us to see more clearly—and then asks us not to mistake the tool for the world.

I recommend that you read it.
